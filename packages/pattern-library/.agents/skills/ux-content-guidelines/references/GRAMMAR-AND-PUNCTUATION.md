# Grammar and Punctuation

> Rules for punctuation, grammar, and sentence structure in GoDaddy product content.

## Serial Commas

We use the serial comma (we deviate from Brand here, following AP Style). Put a comma before "and" in lists.

> **Correct:** You can get an email, a blog, and a domain with this plan.
>
> **Incorrect:** You can get an email, a blog and a domain with this plan.

## Colons

We prefer not to use colons to introduce lists — it's a design choice. Introduce lists with a full sentence without a colon. We do use colons in body copy when it serves our voice and style.

## Ampersands

Write out "and." Avoid using the "&" symbol, including in headlines and other tight spaces. If you need to save space, consider rephrasing the copy instead.

## Dashes

### En Dashes (-)

Used to indicate a range, without a space on either side.

> Mon–Fri 8 am–3 pm MST

### Em Dashes (—)

Used to denote a pause in a sentence, set off extra information, or join two thoughts together.

> Our GoDaddy Guides are here for you 24/7 — just give them a call.
>
> Our GoDaddy Guides — available 24/7 — are waiting for your call.

- Double dashes (--) should never be used. Use an em dash (—) instead.
- On mobile surfaces — to save space — cut the spaces on either side of an em dash.

## Ellipses

Use ellipses only to indicate something is in progress. Don't overuse them. Don't include a space between the end of the text and the ellipses.

> **Correct:** Just a sec while we upload your files...

## Exclamation Points

Exclamation points can come across as shouting or overly friendly. Save them for congratulating a significant accomplishment, not everyday tasks. Use them sparingly.

**Exception:** In chatbots, exclamation points may be used more frequently since they mimic person-to-person conversations.

## Hyphens

Generally, if you're combining two words to describe a third word, hyphenate those first two.

### When to Use

Hyphens create compound adjectives that precede the noun they're describing:
- Auto-renew
- Add-ons
- 30-second video, 2-minute course
- Sell-ready
- Product-level descriptions

### How to Use

**Capitalization:** In title-case contexts (section titles, buttons, nav labels), capitalize the word after the hyphen too.

> **Correct:** Add-Ons
>
> **Incorrect:** Add-ons

**Spacing:** No space before or after a hyphen.

> **Correct:** Get your website sell-ready in just a few steps.
>
> **Incorrect:** Get your website sell - ready in just a few steps.

### When NOT to Use

- Compound adverbs that modify a verb don't get hyphens ("Selling In Person," not "Selling In-Person").
- Adverbs ending in -ly don't need hyphens (quickly, intensely, dynamically).
- We drop hyphens from email, ecommerce, ebook (not e-mail, e-commerce, e-book).

## Periods

Use periods to end complete sentences — unless it's a headline.

### Don't Use Periods In

- One-sentence headlines
- Section titles (they should never be complete sentences)
- CTAs or button copy (they should never be complete sentences)
- Labels
- Bulleted lists
- Hyperlinks

### Use Periods In

- Tooltips
- Body copy
- H2s
- Eyebrows
- Subheads
- Multiple-sentence headlines

### TL;DR

- Single sentence in a headline of any kind = no period
- Single sentence not in a headline = use a period
- No need to double space after a sentence

## Active vs Passive Voice

Use active voice — the subject acts upon the verb, not the other way around.

> **Correct:** You're deleting images from your library.
>
> **Incorrect:** These images will be deleted from your library.

**Exception for "will":** Use "will" when the message denotes something that actually will happen in the future.

> **Correct:** This site will be set up on a temporary domain. *(hasn't happened yet)*
>
> **Incorrect:** This site is set up on a temporary domain. *(not accurate if it hasn't happened yet)*

Remove unnecessary "will" structures:

> **Correct:** You can update your site whenever.
>
> **Incorrect:** You'll be able to edit your site whenever.

## Contractions

We use contractions — they make us sound human and personal.

> "We'll email you" vs "We will email you"

**Informal exception:** "There's" with plural nouns is acceptable.

> There's multiple domains for you to choose from.

Technically it should be "There are," but "There's" is used regularly in conversation and sounds more natural.

## Future Tense

Avoid using "will" unnecessarily.

> **Correct:** You need to save a draft before publishing.
>
> **Incorrect:** You will need to save a draft before publishing.

There are times when you do need it (e.g., "We've fixed the issue and future attachments will be saved."), but remove most instances of the "will" structure.

## Plurals and Possessives

Don't use the extra "s" for plural possessives. Place the apostrophe after the "s."

> **Correct:** customers', business'
>
> **Incorrect:** customers's, business's

When talking about a customer's products where we don't know how many they own, default to plural rather than using parentheses.

> **Correct:** "The following domains are set to renew"
>
> **Incorrect:** "The following domain(s) are set to renew"

## Who vs That

"Who" is for people and "that" is for stuff and animals.

> **Correct:** Entrepreneurs who have more than one domain see 5X the website traffic.
>
> **Incorrect:** Entrepreneurs that have more than one domain see 5X the website traffic.

## Their vs Its

Use "their" when referring to groups of people, like GoDaddy Guides.

> **Correct:** Our GoDaddy Guides, and their decades of website-building experience, are standing by ready to help.
>
> **Incorrect:** Our GoDaddy Guides, and its decades of website-building experience, are standing by ready to help.

## "To" as a Clause

Lead with the "to" clause when presenting a step on how to complete an action, so the outcome comes first.

> **Correct:** To update your login info and password, go to My Account.

This rule doesn't apply for simpler, more logical next steps:

> **Correct:** Click next to save your changes.
>
> **Incorrect:** To save your changes, click next.
