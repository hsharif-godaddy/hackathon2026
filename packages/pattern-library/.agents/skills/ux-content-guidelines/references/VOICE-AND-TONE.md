# Voice and Tone

> How GoDaddy sounds in product experiences — our authentic voice and how tone shifts by context.

## Our Voice: The Doubt Slayer

GoDaddy's voice is unified across all surfaces — brand, product, Front of Site, and Airo. Our brand personality is **the Doubt Slayer**: we turn "too complicated" into "totally doable," reshaping technology to remove uncertainty, confusion, and friction.

Our voice is built on three pillars — **Forward-Thinking**, **Energizing**, and **Demystifying** — defined in detail in the `brand-guidelines` skill (`references/VOICE-AND-TONE.md`). Those pillars apply equally in-product. What this document adds is the practical layer: how tone flexes across product surfaces, and the product-specific goals that shape how we write.

While our voice stays constant, our tone flexes to meet the moment. In product, that flex is guided by the 3 E's below.

### The 3 E's of Experience Design

In product, the UX team has aligned to the 3 E's as goals of experience design. Our voice should serve these goals:

| Goal | Characteristics | Guiding Question |
|------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Efficient** | Concise, simple, effortless | How can we communicate in a way that helps customers achieve things faster? |
| **Effective** | Understanding, responsive, confident | How do we help customers achieve growth, find the right solution, make the right next step? |
| **Enjoyable** | Lighthearted, positive, empowering | How can we build experiences that make tough work less painful and building a business more fun? |

## Our Tone

Tone can shift from moment to moment, adjusting to meet our customers where they are. Like any sage guide, we listen to how the customer is feeling, and we moderate our tone to fit that context.

When thinking about tone, consider:
- The moment in the customer journey
- The mood of the customer
- The type of message we are communicating
- The desired outcome of the experience

### Tone Spectrum: Serious vs Lighthearted

| Serious | Lighthearted |
|---------|-------------|
| **Mood:** Anxious, upset, angry | **Mood:** Excited, delighted, steady |
| **Moments:** Completing hard tasks, delivering bad news, urgent tasks | **Moments:** Announcements, celebrating wins, guiding, happy paths |
| **Surfaces:** Error messages, warning banners, tech failures | **Surfaces:** Flows, success screens, recommendation cards, upgrade suggestions |

### Tone Spectrum: Inspiring vs Matter-of-Fact

| Inspiring | Matter-of-Fact |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Mood:** Curious, hopeful, excited, future-thinking | **Mood:** Uncertain, frustrated, confused, focused |
| **Moments:** Onboarding, introducing new product, celebrating wins | **Moments:** Completing tasks, troubleshooting, problem solving |
| **Surfaces:** Success screens, recommendation cards, upgrade suggestions | **Surfaces:** Instructional copy, tooltips, warning banners, error messages |

### Tone Spectrum: Urgent vs Low Pressure

| Urgent | Low Pressure |
|--------|-------------|
| **Mood:** Busy, inattentive, steady | **Mood:** Excited, delighted, steady |
| **Moments:** Urging action, saving time and headaches | **Moments:** Saving time, FYIs, making recommendations, improving performance |
| **Surfaces:** Warning banners, alerts | **Surfaces:** Announcement banners, notifications, recommendation cards |

## Delivering Bad News

Celebrating our customers is important, but so too is delivering bad news. Tips for doing it well:

- **Be authentic.** Understand and acknowledge the impact the bad news might have on users, and hit the appropriate tone.
- **Apologize, but don't overdo it.** Say you're sorry when something is our fault, but don't burden the user with an overdone apology.
- **Focus on the resolution.** Whether the news was a failure on our part or an inevitable low point, there's usually something we can do. Always include a tip, next step, or offer to help.

## Celebrations

Executing celebratory copy is about matching the tone of the moment. Don't over-celebrate small tasks, and don't miss a moment to congratulate customers on important ones.

**Important distinction:** Don't over-celebrate incremental steps that help users achieve their larger vision, and don't celebrate moments where we completed something that's our job.

> **Incorrect:** Woohoo! You added your first product! This is huge — let's keep going!
>
> **Correct:** Nice job adding your first product — let's keep going.

Consider when you could do better than trite exclamations like "Awesome!", "Great!", or "Nice!". Use language that's more human and matches the gravity of the achievement.

> **Generic:** Your sales are up this month! That's awesome!
>
> **Better:** Selling more this month than you did last month? That's really something to be proud of.
