# Writing Basics

> Foundational principles for writing GoDaddy in-product content.

## Writing for Everyone

We write with purpose. Whether it's to help our users navigate an experience, celebrate success, or find the motivation to keep going, our words are crafted in empathy.

### Accessibility and Inclusion

GoDaddy is a brand that celebrates the diversity of entrepreneurs around the world. In our products, we strive to be accessible to and inclusive of all cultures, races, ages, backgrounds, education, ability, genders, opportunities, and experiences.

- Don't use language that's harmful to anyone based on race or ethnicity, age, gender, or socioeconomic status. Many commonly used phrases have questionable origins. If you plan to use one, ask yourself first if it's common enough that all readers will get the meaning, and second if it's harmless.
- Don't use colloquialisms or idioms that might not be easily understood by all of our users, like those who don't speak English as a first language.
- Avoid long, complex words in favor of common and easy-to-read ones.
- Try to avoid using "disable" or "enable" and opt for less technical and more inclusive language, like "turn on/off."
- For cases that aren't status labels, like "AI-enabled," go for something like "AI-powered."

## How to Write Simply

Our voice is human, clear, and uncomplicated. We achieve that by writing in a natural, conversational way — we get to the point and avoid overwriting.

### Cut Complexity

> **Correct:** We'll email you the info.
>
> **Incorrect:** We will send you an email with the necessary information.

### Keep Sentences Short

Keep things short, sweet, and useful. Remember the economy of words.

### Make It Scannable

Customers are only going to take a few seconds to read and absorb your words. Make sure your copy is easy to understand at first glance.

### Avoid Marketing Words

Robust, bespoke, revolutionary — when's the last time you used these words in a conversation with someone who isn't your enemy? If the answer is never, avoid them.

### Cut Redundancy

> **Correct:** Today, let's focus on these three steps.
>
> **Incorrect:** Today, let's focus on the top three steps to finish today.

Common redundant phrases to avoid:
- "Add an additional"
- "Added bonus"
- "Print out"
- "Click on"

When working on pieces of an experience, it can be easy to fall into redundant use of certain words like "set up," "start," "create," "explore." If you're writing content for a module within a dashboard, review the whole dashboard and cut redundancies, especially where verbs or prompts are concerned.

## Content Checklist

Four steps for getting your content into shippable shape when you don't have the support of a Content Designer:

### 1. Determine what the content needs to accomplish
- What are your key goals for designing this experience?

### 2. Write only that
- In the simplest language you can, communicate that to the customer.

### 3. Consult the UX Content Design Playbook
- Check for errors or jargon.
- Check that the tone is appropriate.
- Check that your content is formatted, punctuated, and capitalized according to our style.

### 4. Polish the copy
- Read it out loud, send it to a colleague.
- You're looking for content that sounds natural, human, and is clear to someone who's not an expert.
- Consult `#contentofficehours` for help polishing.
